Chapter 1 · The gap

The ocean is our largest ecosystem, and the one we understand least

The ocean covers about 70% of the planet and holds 95% of its living space [1], [2]. It is also where human activity leaves the widest footprint: in a single decade our cumulative impact on it nearly doubled, and nearly 60% of the ocean saw those pressures rise [3].

Yet almost none of it is visible to the people whose choices shape it. Ocean science lives in papers, agency datasets, and specialist language. That gap between what scientists know and what the public understands has a real cost. When Florida's 2018 red tide hit, tourism losses reached an estimated $2.7 billion, and some of the largest daily losses came when algae levels were low, driven by public perception rather than the bloom itself [4]. People care about the ocean. What they're missing is a clear picture of it.

Chapter 3 · The method

A patient with several conditions at once isn't cured by treating the loudest symptom. Medicine has a discipline for complex, failing systems, and each step has an ocean equivalent.

Chapter 4 · Guided missions

Four targeted problems, four mission plans

Each mission takes one ocean problem and plans the data collection needed to act on it: objectives, tools, permits, logistics, success measures, and risks. They're planning concepts, written the way I would plan an expedition.

MISSION 01 · TREAT PRECISELY

Find the lionfish front before it moves

Problem: invasive lionfish are spreading along Brazil's coast faster than they can be removed.

Plan: predict where they'll settle next from seafloor structure, then test it at sea with AI-annotated video and eDNA.

 MISSION 02 · DIAGNOSE

From farm field to scallop bed

Problem: North Carolina's bay scallops collapsed, with nitrogen runoff among several suspects.

Plan: follow nitrogen from land to estuary, measure each cause, and forecast what each bloom will cost.

 MISSION 03 · BASELINE

Measure the deep before we disturb it

Problem: decisions on deep-sea mining and trawling are moving faster than the evidence.

Plan: build an open, independent baseline of what lives there and how it recovers.

 MISSION 04 · TREAT AND MONITOR

Plant coral where it can live

Problem: restored coral often dies in the next heatwave.

Plan: screen for climate refuges, prove heat tolerance in the field, and act on heat forecasts before damage.
